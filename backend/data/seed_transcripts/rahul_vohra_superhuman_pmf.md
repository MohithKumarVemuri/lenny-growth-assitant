# Rahul Vohra: The Superhuman Product-Market Fit Engine (Lenny's Podcast)
**Guest:** Rahul Vohra
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Topic:** The 40% PMF Benchmark, Segmenting High-Expectation Customers (HXC), and Prioritizing the Roadmap

---

## Measuring the Unmeasurable: The 40% Rule

[Timestamp: 05:20]
Lenny: "Rahul, before Superhuman launched publicly, you developed a quantitative methodology to measure and optimize product-market fit. How did that begin?"

Rahul Vohra: "When we were two years into building Superhuman, I had a terrifying realization: everyone talks about product-market fit, but nobody could define a metric for it. If you can't measure it, you cannot optimize it.

I discovered Sean Ellis's benchmark from surveying over 100 startups. He asked users one simple question:
**'How would you feel if you could no longer use the product?'**
1. Very disappointed
2. Somewhat disappointed
3. Not disappointed (it isn't really that useful)

Ellis found that across high-growth companies that successfully scaled (like Dropbox, Slack), $> 40\%$ of users answered **'Very Disappointed'**. Companies that struggled consistently scored under 40%."

## Segmenting Your High-Expectation Customer (HXC)

[Timestamp: 15:45]
Rahul Vohra: "When we first ran the survey at Superhuman in 2017, our score was 22%. It was heartbreaking. But here is the secret: you cannot look at the aggregate score.

We segmented our respondents. We asked: 'Who are the people who said Very Disappointed?' They were founders, executives, VC partners, and lawyers—people receiving 400+ emails a day.
When we isolated that specific High-Expectation Customer persona, our score jumped from 22% to 33%. 

Then we looked at the users who answered 'Somewhat Disappointed' and filtered them:
- If what they wanted was opposite to our core vision (e.g., 'Make it look like Apple Mail or add fancy marketing templates'), we politely ignored them.
- If what they loved matched our HXC (e.g., speed, keyboard shortcuts), but they were held back by something fixable (e.g., mobile offline sync), that became our exact product roadmap."

---

## The 50/50 Rule for the Product Roadmap

[Timestamp: 28:10]
Rahul Vohra: "Most founders make the mistake of spending 100% of their time fixing bugs and missing features for unhappy users. That destroys your differentiation.

We instituted the 50/50 rule:
- **50% of our engineering bandwidth** was dedicated to doubling down on what the 'Very Disappointed' users already loved: making keyboard shortcuts faster, adding instant search, optimizing micro-interactions.
- **The other 50%** was dedicated to removing the specific roadblocks for the 'Somewhat Disappointed' users who loved our speed.

Within four quarters of executing this loop, our Product-Market Fit score surged from 22% to 58%, and that is when we scaled."
