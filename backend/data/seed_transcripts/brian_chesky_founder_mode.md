# Brian Chesky: What Founder Mode Really Means (Lenny's Podcast)
**Guest:** Brian Chesky
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky
**Topic:** Founder Mode vs. Manager Mode, Operational Excellence, and Product Reviews

---

## The Conventional Wisdom That Failed

[Timestamp: 04:15]
Lenny: "Brian, your recent speech at Y Combinator about 'Founder Mode' set Silicon Valley on fire. Everyone is debating what you meant. Can you walk us through what led to this realization at Airbnb?"

Brian Chesky: "When Airbnb got bigger, people started telling me: 'Brian, you're running a multi-billion dollar company now. You can't be in the details anymore. You have to hire experienced managers, empower them, delegate everything, and just manage the metrics.' 

So I followed that conventional MBA advice. I hired top executives from all the big tech companies. I stepped out of the product reviews. I told them: 'You run your division.' And you know what happened? It was an absolute disaster.

The product became fragmented. We had 10 different versions of a button. Projects took 18 months instead of 3 weeks. Nobody felt personal accountability because everything was decided by consensus in large committees. When the pandemic hit in 2020 and our revenue dropped 80% in eight weeks, I realized: if we keep running Airbnb like professional managers, we are going to die."

---

## Defining Founder Mode

[Timestamp: 12:40]
Brian Chesky: "So what is Founder Mode? The core premise is that founders have an emotional connection, a biological connection to the company and the product that professional managers almost never have.

Manager Mode says: 'Hire great people and get out of their way. Don't micromanage.' But what that usually means in practice is abdication. You hand off strategy to people who may not deeply care about the company's long-term soul.

Founder Mode means:
1. Staying in the details: As CEO, I am in the design reviews. I review every single product release before it ships to the public. Steve Jobs did this. Walt Disney did this.
2. Removing layers: We eliminated the traditional product management layer and merged PM with Product Marketing. Now our product managers are responsible for both building the product and telling the world about it.
3. Operating on one synchronized roadmap: Instead of 50 decentralized teams shipping conflicting updates, Airbnb now has two major seasonal releases per year—the Summer Release and the Winter Release. The entire company aligns on a single heartbeat."

## Is Founder Mode Just Micromanagement?

[Timestamp: 21:05]
Lenny: "A lot of people push back and say: 'Brian, isn't Founder Mode just an excuse for toxic micromanagement and burning out your team?' How do you draw the line?"

Brian Chesky: "There is a massive difference between micromanagement and having high standards. Micromanagement is telling someone which key on the keyboard to press without giving them context. 

Staying in the details means:
- You inspect the work because you care about excellence.
- You ask: 'Why does this checkout flow have five steps when it could have two?'
- You teach people how to think like founders.

When you run a company with high agency, your best people don't leave because you care about the details; they thrive because they finally work in an organization where excellence is recognized and mediocrity isn't hidden behind corporate bureaucracy."
