## What Does High Agency Really Mean?

[Timestamp: 06:10]
Lenny: "Shreyas, you've talked extensively about 'High Agency' being the single most predictive trait for top 1% product leaders. How do you define High Agency?"

Shreyas Doshi: "High agency is about bending reality to your will. When a low-agency person encounters a barrier—a budget constraint, a stubborn stakeholder, an engineering shortage—they say: 'Well, that's company policy, there's nothing I can do.' They let external circumstances dictate their outcomes.

A high-agency person looks at that same barrier and asks: 'Given this constraint, how do we still achieve the mission?' They question assumptions. They find lateral paths. If door A is locked, they look for door B, and if door B is locked, they climb through the window.

In product management, high agency isn't about being stubborn or aggressive; it's about taking radical accountability for the end outcome regardless of the obstacles."

## The LNO Framework for Peak Effectiveness

[Timestamp: 18:30]
Lenny: "Every product manager struggles with time management and drowning in Slack messages. You introduced the LNO Framework to solve this. Can you break down L, N, and O?"

Shreyas Doshi: "Most PMs treat every task as equally important. They write a 10-page spec with the same perfectionist zeal as replying to an email. That's a recipe for burnout and mediocrity.

The LNO Framework categorizes all work into three buckets:
1. **L = Leverage Tasks:** These are tasks where an extraordinary outcome produces 10x or 100x return. Examples include: defining the 12-month product vision, designing the critical onboarding user journey, hiring your lead architect. On Leverage tasks, you give 100% of your energy, craft, and obsessive attention.
2. **N = Neutral Tasks:** Tasks that must be done well, but doing them exceptionally well provides no extra benefit. Examples: writing routine release notes, status reporting to peers. On Neutral tasks, you aim for good enough (80% effort) and ship.
3. **O = Overhead Tasks:** Administrative friction that creates zero strategic leverage. Examples: filing expense reports, filling out standard compliance checklists, routine calendar scheduling. On Overhead tasks, you aim for minimum acceptable threshold (just don't get fired), batch them, or delegate them.

Top PMs spend 60% of their creative energy on Leverage, 30% on Neutral, and reduce Overhead to 10%."

## Good Product Manager vs. Bad Product Manager

[Timestamp: 32:45]
Shreyas Doshi: "Let me share a few contrasting traits:
- **Bad PMs** think their job is to be the idea generator. They believe they are the 'CEO of the Product'.
- **Good PMs** know their job is to create clarity. They synthesize insights from customers, data, design, and engineering so the best idea wins, regardless of who thought of it.
- **Bad PMs** focus on output: how many features did we ship this sprint?
- **Good PMs** focus obsessively on outcome: did this feature move retention, decrease churn, or solve an acute customer problem?
- **Great PMs run Pre-mortems:** Before launching, they ask the team: 'Imagine it's six months from now and this project was a catastrophic failure. What caused it?' This surfaces hidden organizational anxieties that cheerleading suppresses."
